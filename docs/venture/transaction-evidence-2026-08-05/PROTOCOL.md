## Hypotheses

H1: A small UK AI supplier is responding to a UK general insurer's
production-onboarding evidence request for an AI-assisted claims-triage system.

H0: The exact transaction is not sufficiently evidenced, accessible or
commercially material to justify further Regula-specific investment.

Every component remains unverified. H1 will not be broadened to rescue it. At
most one adjacent transaction may be evaluated where direct evidence establishes
a materially stronger transaction.

## Frozen questions

1. Is the exact H1 transaction demonstrably occurring?
2. What event triggers supplier review?
3. What evidence does the insurer or claims organisation request?
4. Which evidence is mandatory, negotiable or contextual?
5. Who requests, reviews, blocks and approves it?
6. What evidence is difficult for suppliers to produce?
7. Is delay, rejection, rework or cost publicly evidenced?
8. Is there evidence of a budget or purchased substitute?
9. What proportion of requested evidence could Regula observe technically?
10. What proportion requires organisational, contractual, legal or independent evidence?
11. What existing platform, consultancy, assessor or standard already handles the job?
12. Is access to qualified participants realistically obtainable?
13. What would falsify H1?
14. What result would justify owner spend on professional advice and discovery?

## Method and evidence classes

Use multivocal public evidence, retaining source classes separately:
`PRIMARY_BUYER_EVIDENCE`, `PRIMARY_REGULATORY_EVIDENCE`,
`PRIMARY_TRANSACTION_ARTEFACT`, `NAMED_SUPPLIER_EVIDENCE`,
`PEER_REVIEWED_EVIDENCE`, `INDUSTRY_BODY_EVIDENCE`, `VENDOR_CLAIM`,
`USER_REPORTED`, `REASONED`, `UNVERIFIED`, and `CONTRADICTED`.

Official regulation is not transaction demand. Vendor material is not buyer
evidence. Procurement analogues are not private-insurer purchasing. Use-case
existence is not supplier burden. Public research cannot validate payment.

## Search and stopping rules

Begin with official supervisory, insurer, industry, supplier-onboarding,
outsourcing, claims, procurement and named-supplier lanes. Preserve exact query,
UTC time, filters, result/screen/include counts, exclusions, canonical URL,
source class, decisive excerpt, retrieval method and decision effect. Deduplicate
by canonical URL and content hash where practical.

Run at least three materially different rounds. After each, rank unresolved gaps
by decision consequence. Stop after three consecutive rounds add no new direct
transaction artefact, buyer requirement, supplier burden, decision role, payment
signal, contradiction, substitute or access route. This establishes searched
source saturation only.

`SUPPORTED_FOR_DISCOVERY` requires direct buyer workflow, independent supplier
burden, overlapping requirements, plausible participant access, a material
Regula-observable component and no decisive substitute redundancy. Willingness
to pay remains `UNVALIDATED` under every outcome.

## Deviations

The web-research interface did not expose a durable execution timestamp for the
first six search rounds. Their date, order and exact query strings were retained,
but their second-level UTC timestamps were not. From round 7 onward, a separate
UTC timestamp was captured immediately before each search. This limits timing
auditability but does not change the inclusion or decision rules.

The public web interface did not provide stable raw response artefacts for
content hashing or search-engine universe counts. Early-round gap rankings were
not appended to the permanent log after every round. After adversarial review
showed that rounds 8 to 10 had still added generic substitute categories, the
initial saturation assertion was withdrawn and three further materially distinct
rounds were run. Rounds 11 to 13 were the first valid three-round no-new-category
sequence. These deviations limit reproducibility; they do not relax the frozen
decision gate.
