The result is deliberately conservative:

- SPDX and CycloneDX can provide portable component metadata if a named buyer consumes it.
- Signed, version-bound receipts can show that a particular key signed particular content. Trusted identity binding is required to attribute the key to a person or organisation, and a trusted timestamp or transparency record is required to establish time. Signing does not prove that the assertion is true.
- Evidence freshness and change-triggered reopening could reduce stale reuse. This is an unvalidated workflow hypothesis, not defensibility.
- Verifiable credentials and SCITT need issuer, verifier, trust and adoption arrangements that Regula does not have.
- Agent identity and governance are emerging. They do not justify a pivot without observed buyer demand.
- Confidential computing, MPC, zero-knowledge and similar techniques should be deferred until a threat model and actual disclosure refusal require them.
- Article 50 applied from 2 August 2026, with Commission guidelines and a voluntary code published before the cut-off. Timing creates obligations, not product-market fit.

Adopt a standard only when a named buyer or partner consumes it, it reduces measured disclosure or integration cost, it resolves a demonstrated ambiguity, or it enables a cheap external test.

## 11. Relevant theories and their actual decision effect

Four theories change the decision:

1. Jobs to Be Done requires a triggered job, actor and outcome. It rules out “AI governance for SMEs” as an adequate segment description.
2. Information asymmetry and signalling imply that a supplier's self-generated report is cheap to imitate. Buyer-recognised independent review matters more than report volume. Procurement also uses contracts, reputation and audits, so the analogy is incomplete.
3. Transaction-cost economics supports service-first discovery where work is uncertain, expert-dependent and not yet standardised. It does not prove that the work is budgeted.
4. Resource-based and dynamic-capability reasoning places any prospective advantage in lawful accepted/rejected evidence, reviewer calibration and learning, not code, mappings or standards. Access and privacy may prevent that resource from compounding.

Platform economics rejects a passport or exchange until bilateral evidence reuse and adoption are observed. Real-options reasoning supports reversible tests with kill dates. Other theories did not change the recommendation and are omitted.

## 18. Moat and defensibility analysis

There is no current moat. Prospective mechanisms, ranked by replication difficulty, are:

1. Permissioned accepted and rejected evidence examples. Created engagement by engagement; supplier and buyer rights govern use; confidentiality may prevent compounding.
2. Longitudinal evidence-change and decision graph. Compounds only if customers return and permit retention; stale or biased data can degrade it.
3. Independently labelled benchmark corpus. Slow and costly; licensing, privacy and representative sampling are constraints.
4. Reviewer and partner network. Requires trust, quality control, independence and professional-liability management; experts can multi-home.
5. Sector-specific evidence profiles. Replicable unless continually calibrated to real decisions.
6. Workflow integrations and distribution. Switching cost arises only after embedded repeat use.
7. Regulatory currency, standards participation and open-source community. Useful credibility and distribution inputs, but widely available.
8. Software features, mappings and signing. Lowest replication difficulty.

All are prospective. `moat-not-current` in the evidence matrix is the controlling claim.

## 23. Strongest case against the recommendation

The buyer workflow is real, but existing TPRM systems, model-risk platforms, trust centres, standard questionnaires, certifications, auditors and consultants may already handle it adequately. Material insurer reviews require organisational, contractual, security, financial and independently assessed evidence that static code scanning cannot provide. Low-materiality purchases may need only a short form, leaving no valuable middle.

The local-first wedge is replicable and on-premises options already exist. Evidence may be too confidential and buyer-specific to reuse, preventing data effects. Review quality creates liability and expert labour. Buyers may distrust supplier-paid review. The service may become bespoke consulting whose revenue scales with people. With no payment or buyer acceptance, `OPEN_SOURCE_ONLY` or `STOP` may be better outcomes.

## 24. Falsifier

The service-first thesis is falsified if any of the following holds:

- ten qualified supplier and buyer conversations yield no recurring costly workflow or no access to representative inputs;
- five insurer-side reviewers report that current processes are adequate or refuse the proposed evidence boundary;
- a transparent manual baseline is non-inferior on acceptance, effort and error burden;
- no paid validation follows two independently reviewed studies, despite correct target access;
- buyer reviewers reject the pack's usefulness or require assurance outside its boundary;
- human delivery effort does not decline across five comparable engagements;
- evidence cannot be lawfully or usefully reused;
- representative independent labels confirm inadequate technical efficacy;
- professional-liability or data-handling requirements make the economics unacceptable.
