# South Africa's withdrawn draft National AI Policy

**Last updated: 4 August 2026**

South Africa's Draft National Artificial Intelligence Policy was published in [Government Gazette No. 54477 on 10 April 2026](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202604/54477gen3880.pdf). Cabinet subsequently approved its withdrawal after fictitious references were identified, as the [official government news service confirmed on 5 June 2026](https://www.sanews.gov.za/node/81987). The draft is historical material, not current policy or law.

## What the primary records support

- Gazette No. 54477 records publication of the draft and the proposed consultation.
- The 5 June Cabinet statement records Cabinet's approval of the withdrawal and the reason given for it.
- Neither record establishes that a replacement draft, final policy, dedicated AI regulator or AI Act is now in force.

Claims about the withdrawn draft's proposed model or timetable must be labelled as historical. A future replacement should be treated as current only after an official publication is available.

## Existing law still needs a scoped assessment

Withdrawal of the draft does not disapply existing South African law. POPIA may apply where personal information is processed, and section 71 addresses decisions based solely on automated processing in defined circumstances and subject to exceptions. Competition, consumer-protection, copyright, employment and sector-specific rules may also be relevant depending on the facts. This page does not claim that every AI system falls under every listed law.

## Where Regula fits

Regula is primarily an EU AI Act code-indicator and governance-questionnaire tool. It can help inventory systems and flag code patterns for human review. It does not determine how South African law applies, and the withdrawn draft is not a legal-classification source.

Useful commands include:

```bash
pipx install regula-ai
regula discover .
regula check .
regula doctor
```

Scanner output is not a legal opinion or proof of compliance. A South African legal assessment requires the system's actual purpose, data, affected people, organisational role and sector context.
